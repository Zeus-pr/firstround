## Users

Inferred from the approved plan: people with about two or more years of experience who apply to roles and hear almost nothing back. They want a conversation with the person who owns the role, not another place in an applicant queue.

## Product Purpose

First Round finds live roles, names the person who can say yes, and writes a short email in the user's voice. The user reads it and approves it. It then leaves from their own Gmail. A reply stops the follow-ups. Success is a real reply that can become an interview, without the user standing in the public application pile.

## Positioning

The mechanism is a reviewed letter to a named owner of a live role, sent from the user's own inbox. A job board cannot claim that. A mail blaster that sends without a read cannot claim that either.

## Operating Context

Daily pass over licensed job posts. A licensed contact lookup, paid only when an address comes back and verifies. Drafts wait for approval. Up to three follow-ups. The public site, this first surface, only takes a waitlist email. No send, no billing, no resume upload yet.

## Capabilities and Constraints

Confirmed for later slices, not built here: Gmail scopes limited to drafts, send, and threads this app opened. No LinkedIn scraper, no guessed-email blasts, no bought lead lists, no mail from our domain pretending to be the user. One credit is one verified person, one role, one approved first email.

Undecided until the right accounts exist: Railway project ids, the R2 bucket, the exact credit price, Razorpay versus a later Stripe account.

## Brand Commitments

Name: First Round. Domain intent: firstround.space. Typeface: Google Sans, self-hosted, SIL Open Font License. Copy has no em dashes. The public site does not use status pills, eyebrow labels, or a caption under every line. Do not invent customer counts, testimonials, or prices on the page.

## Evidence on Hand

None. No customer replies, no interview counts, no logos. The page may show the mechanism. It may not show proof.

## Product Principles

The user approves every letter before it leaves.
A miss costs the user nothing.
The letter is about one role and one person.
The product speaks in short sentences.
Proof is shown only when it is real.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

The scroll story still reads with reduced motion. The waitlist works from the keyboard. Body text meets contrast on the sheet.
